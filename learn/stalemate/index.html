Stalemate in chess: what it is and how to avoid it

By HussainUpdated October 30, 20266 min read

Stalemate is the chess rule that turns a lot of winning positions into draws. It happens when the player whose turn it is has no legal move, but their king is not in check. Beginners throw away half points to it all the time, usually with an extra queen on the board. Here's how it works and how to make sure it never happens to you.

Quick answer

Stalemate is a draw. It happens when the player to move has no legal moves and their king is not in check. For example, with Black's king on a8, leaves Black with no move at all, so the game is drawn, while would have been checkmate. Each player gets half a point.

How to avoid stalemate

  • Before every move, ask: will my opponent still have a legal move? If the answer is no and you're not giving check, choose another move.
  • Watch the knight's-jump pattern. A queen a knight's jump away from a cornered king, like , is the classic stalemate. is the mate.
  • Bring your king closer first. A queen can't give mate on her own. Leave the enemy king some room while your own king walks over to help.
  • Watch out for blocked pawns. If your opponent's only other pieces are blocked pawns, they can't move either.

Using stalemate to save a lost game

If you're losing badly, stalemate is your friend. Head for the corner with your king, and look for ways to give away your remaining pieces so that your king has no moves left. Strong players set stalemate traps in lost positions all the time, and beginners with a big material lead often fall for them.

Common mistakes

  • Grabbing every last pawn. Taking your opponent's last piece that can move may leave them with no moves at all.
  • Chasing the king with the queen alone. You need your king's help to give mate, so bring it closer.
  • Moving too fast when winning. Most stalemates happen in completely won positions, often in time trouble.

💡 Club-level tip

When you're winning with a queen, slow down at the end. Before each move, count the enemy king's free squares. If the answer would be zero and you're not giving check, stop and pick a different move.

Frequently asked questions

Is stalemate a win or a draw?

A draw. Each player gets half a point, no matter how much material is left on the board.

What is the difference between stalemate and checkmate?

In both, the side to move has no legal move. In checkmate the king is in check, so that player loses. In stalemate the king is not in check, so the game is a draw.

Can it be stalemate with other pieces still on the board?

Yes. If every one of a player's pieces is blocked or pinned and the king has no safe square, it's stalemate, even with plenty of material left.

What if the king has no moves but another piece can move?

Then it is not stalemate and the game goes on. Stalemate only happens when the player has no legal move at all.

Is stalemate a draw under official rules?

Yes. Under the official FIDE rules, and US Chess rules, stalemate ends the game in a draw.
