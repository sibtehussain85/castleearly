Smothered mate: checkmate with a single knight

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 13, 20268 min read

A smothered mate is one of the prettiest checkmates in chess: a lone knight gives check, and the king can't move because its own pieces are standing on every escape square. It often comes with a queen sacrifice, and it also turns up in a few well-known opening traps. Here is the pattern, how to set it up, and how to stop it happening to you.

Quick answer

A smothered mate is a checkmate delivered by a knight when the enemy king has no free squares because its own pieces surround it. A knight's check can't be blocked, so if the knight can't be captured, the game is over. The classic example is a king on h8 hemmed in by a rook on g8 and pawns on g7 and h7: a knight landing on f7 is checkmate, as in .

What is a smothered mate?

Look at Black's king on h8. It has three neighbouring squares, g8, g7 and h7, and every one of them is occupied by a black piece. The king is safe from most attacks behind that wall, but it also can't move at all. It has been smothered.

Now White plays . There are only three ways to answer a check: move the king, capture the checking piece, or block the check. The king can't move, nothing attacks f7, and a knight's check can never be blocked, because the knight jumps. That's checkmate, with a single piece.

❓ Quick check

Why can a check from a knight never be blocked?

A queen, rook or bishop checks along a line, so a piece can step in between. A knight jumps straight to its square, so there is nothing to block.

Philidor's legacy, step by step

You'll rarely find the king already boxed in. Usually you have to force the last blocking piece onto the board, and the best-known way to do it is called Philidor's legacy. You need a queen and a knight near the enemy king, and the enemy king on g8 with its g- and h-pawns still at home.

  1. Check on the diagonal. drives the king to h8. (After 1...Kf8, 2.Qf7 is mate.)
  2. Knight to f7, then double check. After Kg8, the knight jumps away with . The knight and the queen both give check, so Black can't block or capture: the king must go back to h8.
  3. Sacrifice the queen. looks like a blunder, but the knight on h6 protects the queen, so the king can't capture. Black must play ...Rxg8.
  4. Knight back to f7. . The rook that just took your queen is now part of the wall.

The pattern is named after the French master François-André Philidor, but it is much older: it already appears in Luis Ramírez de Lucena's chess book of 1497, one of the oldest printed chess books that survives.

❓ Quick check

In Philidor's legacy, why can't the king simply capture the queen on g8?

The knight on h6 guards g8, and a king can never capture a protected piece. So the rook has to take, and that smothers the king.

Smothered mate in the opening: three traps

Smothered mates don't only happen in the corner. In the opening, the king is still in the middle with its own queen, bishops and pawns packed around it, and sometimes a pawn that could capture the knight is pinned. Three traps are worth knowing.

1. The Caro-Kann trap: 6.Nd6#

2. The Budapest Gambit trap: 8...Nd3#

The pinned-pawn version

All three opening traps share one idea: a pawn that could take the knight is pinned. The same thing happens in the corner. Here is checkmate even though the h7-pawn attacks g6, because that pawn is pinned to its king by the rook on h1. When you see a boxed-in king, check whether the pieces guarding the knight's square are really free to move.

How to spot a smothered mate

  • The enemy king has few or no free squares, and its own pieces are the reason.
  • You have a knight within one or two jumps of a checking square (f7, g6, h6 against a castled king; d6, d3, f3, f6 against a king in the middle).
  • The checking square can't be captured, or the piece guarding it is pinned.
  • One square is still free? Look for a sacrifice that forces an enemy piece onto it, like Qg8+ in Philidor's legacy.

How to avoid being smothered

  • Give your king air. A quiet pawn move like h3 or h6 (often called “luft”) gives the king an escape square and ends most smothered-mate ideas.
  • Watch for a queen and knight together near your king. They are the two pieces that work best as a team.
  • Be careful with Kh8 or Kh1 when an enemy knight can reach f7 or f2 with check.
  • Check your pinned pawns. In the Caro-Kann and Budapest traps the losing side had a pawn attacking the knight's square, but it was pinned.
  • Don't grab “free” pieces without looking. In two of the three traps above, the losing move was a greedy capture.

💡 Club-level tip

When your opponent's knight comes close to your king, count your king's free squares before you do anything else. If the answer is zero, make an escape square now. Frequently asked questions

What is a smothered mate in chess?

A checkmate given by a knight when the enemy king cannot move because its own pieces occupy every square around it. Since a knight check cannot be blocked, the king is mated if the knight cannot be captured.

Why is it called a smothered mate?

Because the king is “smothered” by its own pieces. They stand on all of its escape squares, so it has no room to move when the knight gives check.

What is Philidor's legacy?

The best-known way to force a smothered mate. The attacker checks with the queen, gives a double check with the knight, sacrifices the queen on g8 so that a rook has to capture, and then mates with the knight on f7. It is named after François-André Philidor, although it appears in Lucena's book of 1497.

Can a smothered mate happen in the opening?

Yes. Well-known examples are 6.Nd6# in the Caro-Kann Defense, 8...Nd3# in the Budapest Gambit and 7...Nf3# in the Blackburne Shilling trap. In each one a pawn or piece that could capture the knight is pinned.

Which piece gives a smothered mate?

Always a knight. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces, so it is the only one that can check a king that is completely surrounded.

How do you prevent a smothered mate?

Give your king an escape square with a pawn move such as h3 or h6, keep an eye on enemy knights near your king, and check whether the pawns guarding the knight’s checking square are pinned.

Is a smothered mate always in the corner?

No. The corner is the most common place, but any king that is fully blocked by its own pieces can be smothered, including a king still on e1 or e8 in the opening.

Do you have to sacrifice the queen for a smothered mate?

No. The queen sacrifice is only needed when one square next to the king is still free and you have to force an enemy piece onto it. If the king is already boxed in, the knight check alone is mate.
