How a chess game ends in a draw: the five draw rules

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

Not every chess game has a winner. A game can end level in five different ways, and knowing them matters in both directions: they stop you from throwing away a win, and they give you a way to save a game you were losing.

Quick answer

A chess game is a draw in five ways: stalemate (the player to move has no legal move and is not in check), threefold repetition (the same position occurs three times), the 50-move rule (50 moves by each side with no capture and no pawn move), insufficient material (neither side can possibly checkmate) and agreement between the players. A draw scores half a point for each player.

1. Stalemate

If it is your turn, your king is not in check, and you have no legal move with any piece, the game is drawn. It doesn't matter how much material the other side has.

Stalemate is the draw beginners give away most often, usually with an extra queen on the board. It is also a defensive weapon: in this puzzle White is losing badly, and a queen sacrifice forces stalemate.

2. Threefold repetition

If the same position occurs three times in a game, either player can claim a draw. The positions don't have to come one after another. They count as the same when the same player is to move, every piece is on the same square, and the same moves are possible, including castling rights and en passant captures.

Over the board you claim the draw yourself. Online, sites such as Chess.com and Lichess end the game for you. And if the position occurs five times, the game is drawn automatically under FIDE rules, with no claim needed.

The most common cause is perpetual check: one side keeps checking and the king can't escape, so the position repeats. Try it here.

3. The 50-move rule

If both players make 50 moves in a row without any capture and without any pawn move, either player can claim a draw. Every capture and every pawn move sets the count back to zero.

The rule stops a player from playing on forever in a position they can't win. It matters most in endgames such as king, bishop and knight against king, where the checkmate is possible but takes many accurate moves.

If nobody claims and the count reaches 75 moves each, the game is drawn automatically.

4. Insufficient material

When neither player has enough pieces left to give checkmate by any series of legal moves, the game ends in a draw at once. The official rules call this a dead position. These endings are always drawn:

  • King against king
  • King and bishop against king
  • King and knight against king
  • King and bishop against king and bishop, when both bishops move on the same color squares

Two knights are a special case. King and two knights against a lone king can't force checkmate, but a mate is possible if the defender blunders, so the game is not drawn automatically.

The same idea applies to the clock. If your opponent's time runs out but you have no possible way to checkmate, for example you have only a king, the game is a draw and not a win.

5. Draw by agreement

At any point a player can offer a draw. Make your move, say "draw?" or press the draw button, and your opponent can accept or play on. If they accept, the game is over.

Offer a draw only when the position is really level or you are in trouble. Repeating the offer again and again is considered bad manners, and many tournaments don't allow draw offers before a set number of moves.

How to use the draw rules

  • When you are winning, watch for stalemate and for perpetual check. Leave the enemy king a move until you give mate, and keep your own king sheltered from checks.
  • When you are losing, look for a perpetual check, a stalemate trick, or a way to trade down to an ending your opponent can't win, such as king and knight against king.
  • When the position is level, repeating moves is a normal and fair way to end the game.

Frequently asked questions

How many ways can a chess game end in a draw?

Five: stalemate, threefold repetition, the 50-move rule, insufficient material and agreement between the players.

After how many moves is a chess game a draw?

There is no general limit. A draw can be claimed after 50 moves by each side with no capture and no pawn move, and it becomes automatic after 75.

Is stalemate a draw or a win?

A draw. Each player gets half a point, however much material is on the board.

How does threefold repetition work?

If the same position, with the same player to move and the same possible moves, occurs three times in the game, either player can claim a draw.

Is king against king a draw?

Yes. Neither side can ever give checkmate, so the game is drawn immediately.

Can you checkmate with a king and two knights?

You can't force it against correct defense, but checkmate is possible if the defender makes a mistake, so the game is not an automatic draw.

Can you win on time with only a king?

No. If your opponent runs out of time and you have no way to checkmate, the game is a draw.

Who gets the point in a draw?

Both players get half a point.
