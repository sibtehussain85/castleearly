Pawn promotion: what happens when a pawn reaches the end

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20264 min read

The pawn is the weakest piece on the board, with one exception: if it reaches the far side, it turns into something much stronger. That is promotion, and the fight to promote a pawn decides most endgames.

Quick answer

Pawn promotion happens when a pawn reaches the last rank (the 8th for White, the 1st for Black). It must be replaced in the same move by a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same color. The choice is yours and does not depend on which pieces have been captured, so you can have two or more queens.

How promotion works

Move the pawn onto the last rank, take it off the board, and put the new piece on the same square. All of that is one move. The new piece works at once: if it attacks the enemy king, it is check, and it can even be checkmate.

A pawn can also promote by capturing. If it takes a piece on the last rank, it promotes on the square where it captured.

On this board, push the b-pawn one square and choose a piece.

The promotion rules

  1. Promotion is compulsory. A pawn can't stay a pawn on the last rank, and it can't wait a move.
  2. You choose from four pieces: queen, rook, bishop or knight. You can't promote to a king or to another pawn.
  3. The new piece is your own color.
  4. Captured pieces don't matter. You are not limited to pieces you have lost. With all eight pawns promoted you could have nine queens.
  5. The move is finished when the new piece touches the square. In a tournament, if you don't have a spare queen at hand, you may stop the clock and ask the arbiter for one.

A common myth is that an upside-down rook counts as a queen. In casual games people do this all the time, but under the official rules an upside-down rook is still a rook.

Promoting with a capture

Here the white pawn has two ways to promote. It can step forward to b8, or it can capture the rook on a8 and promote there. Capturing is clearly better: you win a rook and get a new queen in the same move.

Which piece should you choose?

Choose the queen almost every time. She is the strongest piece and does everything a rook or a bishop can do.

Picking anything else is called underpromotion, and there are two real reasons for it:

  • A knight moves in a way the queen can't. If promoting to a knight gives check or a fork, it can be the stronger choice.
  • A rook or bishop avoids stalemate in the rare positions where a new queen would leave your opponent with no legal move.

How to get a pawn promoted

  • Create a passed pawn. A passed pawn has no enemy pawn in front of it or on the files beside it, so only pieces can stop it.
  • Support it with your king. In king and pawn endgames, the king walks in front of the pawn and clears the way. Practice this in the endgame drills.
  • Put rooks behind passed pawns. A rook behind the pawn pushes it forward and gains space with every step.
  • Use tactics. A pawn breakthrough or an interference can force a pawn through even when it looks blocked.

How promotion is written

Write the pawn move, then an equals sign and the letter of the new piece: e8=Q means a pawn moved to e8 and became a queen. With a capture it looks like bxa8=Q. If the new piece gives check or mate, add + or # as usual: e8=Q+, f8=N#. See chess notation for the full system.

Frequently asked questions

What happens when a pawn reaches the other side of the board?

It promotes. In the same move it is replaced by a queen, rook, bishop or knight of the same color, chosen by the player.

Can you have two queens in chess?

Yes. Promotion does not depend on which pieces have been captured, so you can have two queens or more.

Can a pawn promote to a king?

No. The only choices are queen, rook, bishop and knight.

Is pawn promotion mandatory?

Yes. A pawn that reaches the last rank must be promoted immediately as part of that move.

Can you promote a pawn to a piece that has not been captured?

Yes. You may choose any of the four pieces whether or not you have lost one.

How many queens can you have in chess?

Up to nine: your original queen plus one for each of your eight pawns.

Can a pawn promote and give checkmate in the same move?

Yes. The new piece takes effect immediately, so a promotion can be check or checkmate.
