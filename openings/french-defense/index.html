French Defense: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20267 min read

The French Defense starts with 1...e6 and 2...d5. Black builds a solid pawn chain and challenges White's center right away. The price is the light-squared bishop on c8, which often gets stuck behind the e6 pawn. French players are known for patient, counterattacking chess.

The main line, move by move

The Advance Variation () is the most common choice at club level. Step through it, then practice Black's moves.

How the French works

Black's pawns on d5 and e6 are very solid. White usually gets more space, but Black has clear targets: the d4 pawn (attacked with ...c5, ...Nc6, ...Qb6) and the e5 pawn (attacked with ...f6).

❓ Quick check

In the Advance French, which White pawn does Black attack first?

d4 is the base of the chain. ...c5, ...Nc6 and ...Qb6 all hit it.

Black's plan

  • Attack the base of the chain with ...c5.
  • Attack the head with ...f6 once your pieces are ready.
  • Find a job for the c8 bishop: ...b6 and ...Ba6, or ...Bd7.

❓ Quick check

Which Black piece is the French's "problem piece"?

The e6 pawn blocks the c8 bishop, so French players plan how to activate or trade it.

The Classical Variation: 3.Nc3 Nf6

Against , Black can develop the knight and pressure e4. After White closes the center with e5, Black uses the same plan: ...c5 and ...f6.

Common beginner mistakes in the French

  • Forgetting ...c5. Without it, Black has no counterplay and gets squeezed.
  • Leaving the c8 bishop as a spectator. Plan a way to trade it or activate it.
  • Castling kingside into a big attack when White has pawns on e5 and f4 and pieces aimed at h7.

💡 Club-level tip

A lot of beginners push e5 as White and then attack without a plan. Stay calm, hit d4 with ...c5, ...Nc6 and ...Qb6, and their big center often starts to crumble on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Is the French Defense good for beginners?

Yes, if you like solid positions. The pawn structure gives clear plans, and you don't need to memorize as much as in the Sicilian.

What's the difference between the French and the Caro-Kann?

Both play ...d5 on move two. In the Caro-Kann, Black first plays ...c6, so the c8 bishop can get out before ...e6. In the French, the bishop is often stuck behind e6.
