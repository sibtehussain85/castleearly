Petrov Defense: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20266 min read

The Petrov Defense, also called the Russian Game, answers 2.Nf3 with 2...Nf6. Instead of defending e5, Black counterattacks e4. It's famous for being very solid and is a favorite of top players who want a safe game with Black. Just don't copy White's moves blindly: there's a well-known trap.

How the Petrov works

The Petrov is about balance. Black gives White nothing to attack, develops quickly and keeps a symmetrical pawn structure. It's hard to beat, which is why many top players use it in important games.

Black's plan

  • Win back the e4 pawn only after ...d6.
  • Keep the knight on e4 supported with ...d5.
  • Develop quickly and castle; look for ...c6 and ...Bf5 setups.

Common beginner mistakes in the Petrov

  • Taking on e4 before ...d6, as shown in the trap above.
  • Copying White forever. Symmetry works for a while, but you must check for tactics every move.
  • Playing too passively. The Petrov is solid, but you still need active piece play to win.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Petrov Defense good for beginners?

Yes, as long as you learn the move order. Its biggest danger is the early trap shown above.

Why is it called the Russian Game?

It's named after Alexander Petrov, a 19th-century Russian player who analyzed it with Carl Jaenisch.
