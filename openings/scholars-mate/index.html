Scholar's Mate: the 4-move checkmate and how to stop it

By HussainUpdated October 30, 20266 min read

Scholar's Mate is the four-move checkmate almost every chess player runs into in their first games. White's queen and bishop team up against the f7 pawn, and if Black doesn't notice, it's checkmate on move 4. Here's exactly how it works and, more importantly, how to stop it every time.

Quick answer

Scholar's Mate is a four-move checkmate: . White's queen and bishop both attack f7, a square that only Black's king defends, so the queen captures there with checkmate. To stop it, block the queen with (or defend f7 with ), then chase the queen away with ...Nf6.

Should you play Scholar's Mate?

Against someone who has only just learned the rules, it wins a lot of games. Against anyone who knows the defense, it backfires. Black blocks with ...g6, develops with ...Nf6 and keeps hitting the queen, and White has spent three or four moves on one piece. Stockfish rates the position after as slightly better for Black.

If you like the idea of attacking f7, learn the Italian Game or the Fried Liver Attack instead. They use the same Bc4 idea, but with proper development, so they still work against stronger players.

Common mistakes

  • Attacking the queen instead of defending f7. looks active, but it's checkmate next move.
  • Only looking at the e5 pawn. Qh5 attacks e5 too, and many beginners defend e5 and forget about f7.
  • Bringing your own queen out too early. If you try Scholar's Mate and it doesn't work, your queen becomes a target. Develop your knights and bishops first.

💡 Club-level tip

Every time your opponent's queen moves, take two seconds to check every square it attacks. That one habit stops Scholar's Mate, Fool's Mate and most early queen tricks.

Frequently asked questions

How many moves is Scholar's Mate?

Four moves: 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nc6 3.Qh5 Nf6?? 4.Qxf7#. White gives checkmate on move 4.

Is Scholar's Mate the fastest checkmate?

No. Fool's Mate is faster: it ends on move 2. Scholar's Mate is simply the most common quick checkmate between beginners.

How do you stop Scholar's Mate?

Defend f7 before it is attacked twice. When the queen is on h5, play ...g6; when it goes to f3, play ...Nf6. ...Qe7 also defends f7.

Is it bad to play Scholar's Mate?

It isn't against the rules or bad manners. It's just a poor plan against players who know the defense, because the early queen gets chased and you fall behind in development.

Can Black do Scholar's Mate?

Yes, if White is careless. The same pattern works against f2, with Black's queen and c5 bishop both aiming at it. The defense for White is the same idea: cover f2 in time.
