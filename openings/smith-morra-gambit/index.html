Smith-Morra Gambit: a gambit against the Sicilian

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

♟ Play this opening vs the Coach Free · every move explained

Tired of learning long Sicilian theory? The Smith-Morra Gambit cuts it short. White gives a pawn on move three, gets rapid development and two open files, and reaches the same kind of position game after game.

Quick answer

The ideas behind the Smith-Morra

White's compensation is time and open lines. After the pawn disappears, White's knight, bishops, queen and rooks all have good squares waiting for them. Black's queen, on the other hand, sits on the open d-file across from a white rook and often has trouble finding a safe home.

White's plan

  • Use the standard setup: Nf3, Bc4, O-O, Qe2, Rd1.
  • Push e5 when it opens the center against Black's king.
  • Put rooks on the c- and d-files and jump a knight to b5 or d5.

Black's plan

  • Develop carefully with ...Nc6, ...d6, ...e6 and castle.
  • Play ...e5 or ...a6 in time to control the b5 and e5 squares.
  • Or decline with 3...Nf6 and avoid the gambit.

❓ Quick check

What is White's standard setup in the Smith-Morra?

The bishop goes to c4, the queen to e2 and a rook to d1, facing Black's queen.

The Siberian Trap

White has to know one trap that works for Black. In the Siberian Variation, Black sets up a queen on c7 and a knight on g4, both aiming at h2. If White carelessly chases the knight with h3, a second knight move wins on the spot.

Common mistakes in the Smith-Morra

  • As Black, playing ...Nf6 too early. After White has . If Black takes with , the queens come off with and a knight lands on b5 with threats Black can't meet comfortably. Play ...e6 first.
  • As White, falling for the Siberian Trap. When Black's queen is on c7 and a knight comes to g4, don't play h3.
  • As White, trading pieces. You are a pawn down. Every exchange brings Black closer to a winning endgame, so keep the pieces on and keep the initiative.

❓ Quick check

In the Siberian Trap, why is 9.h3 a mistake?

After ...Nd4 the knight on f3 is attacked. If it takes on d4, it no longer guards h2 and the queen mates there.

💡 Club-level tip

The Smith-Morra scores best below about 1800, where Black often doesn't know the defensive setups. Memorize the piece placement, not the move orders: Bc4, Qe2, Rd1, and then look for e5.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Smith-Morra Gambit sound?

Engines give Black a small edge with perfect defense, but in practice White scores well at club level because the attack is easy to play and the defense is not.

How do you refute the Smith-Morra Gambit?

There is no simple refutation. Black's most reliable choices are the Classical setup with ...Nc6, ...d6, ...e6 and ...a6, or declining with 3...Nf6.

Should Black accept the Smith-Morra Gambit?

Accepting is the critical test and is fine if you know a defensive setup. If you don't, 3...Nf6 is a safe way to decline.

Who is the Smith-Morra Gambit named after?

Pierre Morra of France and Ken Smith of the United States, who both played and wrote about it in the 20th century.

What is the Siberian Trap?

A trap for Black in the Smith-Morra: with the queen on c7 and a knight on g4, the move ...Nd4 wins because ...Qh2 is checkmate if White captures.
