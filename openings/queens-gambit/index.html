Queen's Gambit: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20268 min read

The Queen's Gambit is one of the oldest and most important openings for White after 1.d4. White offers the c-pawn to pull Black's d5 pawn away from the center. It isn't a real sacrifice: White usually wins the pawn back easily. The Netflix series named after it brought a wave of new players to chess.

Why it's called a gambit

offers a pawn, but if Black takes with , White can usually win it back with e3 and Bxc4. Trying to hold on to the extra pawn with ...b5 tends to weaken Black's queenside.

❓ Quick check

If Black takes with 2...dxc4, how does White usually win the pawn back?

e3 opens the f1 bishop, which simply takes on c4 while developing.

White's plan

  • Keep pressure on d5 with the c-pawn, knight and bishop.
  • Use the c-file for the rooks.
  • Choose between a minority attack on the queenside (b4–b5) and central play with e4.

If Black accepts: 2...dxc4

In the Queen's Gambit Accepted, Black takes the pawn and gives up the center for a while. White simply develops and wins it back.

Common beginner mistakes in the Queen's Gambit

  • Falling for the Elephant Trap. In the Declined, after ...Nbd7, cxd5 exd5 Nxd5?? loses a piece. See the Queen's Gambit Declined lesson.
  • Rushing to win back the pawn with an early queen move after . Develop first; the pawn won't run away.
  • Forgetting the c1 bishop. Develop it before e3 locks it in, or plan for it to go to b2.

❓ Quick check

Why develop the c1 bishop before playing e3?

Once the pawn is on e3, the c1 bishop has no way out to f4 or g5.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Queen's Gambit really a gambit?

Only in name. White can almost always win the pawn back, so it isn't a true sacrifice.

Should I play the Queen's Gambit or the London System?

The London is easier to learn because you use one setup against everything. The Queen's Gambit is more ambitious and teaches you more about central play.
