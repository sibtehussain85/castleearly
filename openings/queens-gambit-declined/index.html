Queen's Gambit Declined: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20267 min read

The Queen's Gambit Declined is Black's most classical answer to 1.d4 d5 2.c4. Black supports the d5 pawn with ...e6 and keeps a firm grip on the center. It has been trusted by world champions for over a century, and it includes one of the most famous traps in chess: the Elephant Trap.

Quick answer

The Queen's Gambit Declined starts 1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6. Black keeps a strong pawn on d5 instead of taking on c4, then develops with ...Nf6, ...Be7 and ...O-O. It's one of the most reliable defenses against 1.d4 and has been played by world champions for more than a century.

The main line, move by move

The Tartakower setup with ...h6 and ...b6 is a reliable choice. Step through it, then practice Black's moves.

How the Queen's Gambit Declined works

Black's pawns on d5 and e6 are very solid. The only real problem is the c8 bishop, which is blocked by e6. Good QGD players always know how they'll activate it: with ...b6 and ...Bb7, or by freeing the position with ...dxc4 and ...c5 or ...e5.

❓ Quick check

Which Black piece is the QGD's "problem piece"?

The e6 pawn blocks it. Good QGD players always know how they'll activate it.

Black's plan

  • Hold d5 firmly and castle.
  • Solve the c8 bishop with ...b6 and ...Bb7.
  • Free the position with ...c5 or ...e5.

The Elephant Trap

This is a classic trap for White players who think the d5 knight is pinned. It isn't: the trap costs White a piece. Practice it as Black.

❓ Quick check

After 6.Nxd5?? Nxd5 7.Bxd8, what does Black play?

7...Bb4+! forces Qd2 Bxd2+ Kxd2 Kxd8, and Black is a piece up.

Common beginner mistakes in the QGD

  • Forgetting the c8 bishop. Have a plan for it from the start.
  • Taking on c4 too early without a follow-up. Usually it's best after White has moved the f1 bishop, to win a tempo.
  • Staying passive. The QGD is solid, but you need ...c5 or ...e5 at some point.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Queen's Gambit Declined good for beginners?

Yes. It follows the opening principles, it's very solid, and the main ideas are easy to learn.

Why is it called the Elephant Trap?

Nobody is completely sure, but the name has been used for over a century for this trap, where White grabs a pawn on d5 and loses a piece.
