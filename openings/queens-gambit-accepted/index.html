Queen's Gambit Accepted: take the pawn, then give it back

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

In the Queen's Gambit Accepted Black simply takes the pawn White offers. Black can't keep it, and shouldn't try. The point is different: while White spends time winning the pawn back, Black develops freely and attacks White's center with ...c5.

The ideas behind the Queen's Gambit Accepted

In the Queen's Gambit Declined, Black keeps a pawn on d5 and often has a passive light-squared bishop. The Accepted is the opposite approach. Black lets White have the center for a few moves, develops without any cramped pieces, and then attacks that center with ...c5 and sometimes ...e5.

White's plan

  • Win the pawn back with e3 and Bxc4 (or the sharper 3.e4).
  • Use the extra space and the isolated d-pawn to attack on the kingside.
  • Push d5 at the right moment to open the position.

Black's plan

  • Play ...c5, then ...a6 and ...b5 to develop the bishop to b7.
  • Trade on d4 to give White an isolated pawn, then blockade it with a knight on d5.
  • Exchange pieces: the isolated pawn gets weaker as the endgame comes closer.

❓ Quick check

What is Black's key pawn move in the Queen's Gambit Accepted?

...c5 attacks the d4 pawn and gives Black's pieces room.

The trap: don't hold the pawn with ...b5

Every beginner tries to keep the extra pawn with ...b5. It doesn't work. White undermines the pawn chain with a4, and when the a-file and the long diagonal open, the queen comes to f3 and attacks the rook on a8. Black has to give up a piece to save it.

Common mistakes in the Queen's Gambit Accepted

  • Holding the pawn with ...b5. As the trap above shows, it usually costs material. Give the pawn back and develop.
  • Forgetting ...c5. If Black never challenges d4, White just has a bigger center for free.
  • As White, rushing to recapture with the queen. Qa4+ wins the pawn back at once but puts the queen where Black can gain time on her. e3 and Bxc4 is simpler.

❓ Quick check

After 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.e3, why is 3...b5 a mistake?

After a4, ...c6, axb5, ...cxb5, the move Qf3 attacks the rook on a8 and wins a piece.

💡 Club-level tip

Think of 2...dxc4 as a loan, not a gift. You are trading the pawn for a few easy developing moves. Players who understand that get comfortable positions with the Accepted for years.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Queen's Gambit Accepted good?

Yes. It is a sound, respected defense that has been used at world championship level. Black gets free development in exchange for giving White the center for a while.

Should you accept the Queen's Gambit?

You can. Accepting with 2...dxc4 and declining with 2...e6 or 2...c6 are all good. Accept if you like open positions, and decline if you prefer a solid center.

Can Black keep the pawn in the Queen's Gambit Accepted?

No. Trying to hold it with ...b5 runs into a4 and Qf3, and Black loses material. That is why the Queen's Gambit is not a true gambit.

What is the best reply to the Queen's Gambit Accepted?

The main moves are 3.Nf3, followed by e3 and Bxc4, and the sharper 3.e4, which takes the full center immediately.

What is the difference between the Queen's Gambit Accepted and Declined?

In the Accepted Black takes on c4 and gives up the center pawn on d5. In the Declined Black keeps the pawn on d5 and supports it with ...e6.
