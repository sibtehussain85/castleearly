Scandinavian Defense: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20266 min read

The Scandinavian Defense, also called the Center Counter, answers 1.e4 with 1...d5, attacking White's pawn immediately. Black gets a simple, easy-to-learn position with a clear structure. The queen comes out early, but on a5 or d6 she's surprisingly safe.

Quick answer

The Scandinavian Defense starts . Black challenges e4 immediately; after the queen moves again, usually to a5 or d6. It's easy to learn because White's first moves are very predictable.

The main line, move by move

The ...Qa5 line is the classic setup. Step through it, then practice Black's moves.

Why play the Scandinavian?

There's very little theory: White can't avoid it after , and Black's setup is almost the same in every game. The structure (pawns on c6 and e6) is like a Caro-Kann, but Black has already traded off White's e-pawn.

Black's plan

  • Get the c8 bishop out to f5 or g4 before ...e6.
  • Play ...c6 to give the queen a safe retreat.
  • Castle and play for ...c5 or ...e5 later.

❓ Quick check

Why does Black play ...c6 in the ...Qa5 line?

With c7 available, the queen can't get trapped on a5.

The modern ...Qd6 line

Many players now retreat the queen to d6. It's flexible and avoids some of White's tricks against the queen on a5.

Common beginner mistakes in the Scandinavian

  • Moving the queen again and again. One retreat is fine; after that, develop other pieces.
  • Forgetting ...c6. Without it, the queen on a5 can get trapped after b4 or Nd5 tricks.
  • Locking in the bishop by playing ...e6 before ...Bf5 or ...Bg4.

❓ Quick check

After 3.Nc3, what's the problem with Black's queen on d5?

The knight gains time by attacking the queen. Move it once to a good square, then develop.

💡 Club-level tip

After , plenty of beginners don't take on d5 and play e5 or Nc3 instead. No problem: get your bishop to f5 or g4 and play normal, healthy chess.

Frequently asked questions

Isn't it bad to bring the queen out early?

Usually, yes. In the Scandinavian, Black loses one tempo, but in return White's e-pawn is gone and Black gets a solid, easy position.

Is the Scandinavian Defense good for beginners?

It's one of the best. You learn one setup and can play it in almost every game against .
