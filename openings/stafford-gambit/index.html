Stafford Gambit: the traps and how to refute it

By HussainUpdated October 30, 20267 min read

How White should refute the Stafford Gambit

The best answer to a trap-filled gambit is almost always the same: don't grab more, don't pin, develop and castle. You're already a pawn up.

Is the Stafford Gambit good?

Not objectively. If White just develops, Stockfish gives White a clear advantage after . But it scores very well in fast online games because its traps are so natural. If you like the Petrov but want something sound, learn the real Petrov Defense: after 3.Nxe5 it continues 3...d6, not 3...Nc6.

Common mistakes

  • As White, pinning with Bg5. It's the exact move that loses to ...Nxe4.
  • As White, answering ...Qh4 with g3. The knight jumps into g3 and a rook falls. Defend with Qe2 instead.
  • As Black, playing only for traps. If White plays d3, Be2 and castles, you're simply a pawn down. Learn the normal plans too.

💡 Club-level tip

Against the Stafford, remember three calm moves: d3, Be2 and castle. Play them and most of the traps simply disappear.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Stafford Gambit sound?

No. With calm play White keeps an extra pawn and a better position. It is popular in blitz because its traps are so easy to fall into.

What is the Stafford Gambit trap?

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 Nc6 4.Nxc6 dxc6 5.d3 Bc5 6.Bg5?? Nxe4! 7.Bxd8 Bxf2+ 8.Ke2 Bg4#.

How do you refute the Stafford Gambit?

After 4...dxc6, play 5.d3, then Be2 and castle. Avoid Bg5, and against ...Qh4 ideas defend with Qe2 instead of g3.

Who made the Stafford Gambit popular?

It became famous through online chess videos and streams, especially those of Eric Rosen.

Is the Stafford Gambit the same as the Petrov?

It starts from the Petrov Defense (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6), but after 3.Nxe5 the real Petrov continues 3...d6. 3...Nc6 is the Stafford Gambit.
