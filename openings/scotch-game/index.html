Scotch Game: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20267 min read

The Scotch Game opens the center right away with 3.d4. Instead of slow maneuvering, White trades a central pawn early and gets active, easy-to-develop pieces. It's named after a correspondence match between Edinburgh and London in the 1820s, and Garry Kasparov brought it back to top-level chess in the 1990s.

Quick answer

The Scotch Game starts . White opens the center at once, and after gets free piece play and a strong pawn on e4. It's a direct, easy-to-learn alternative to the Italian Game and the Ruy Lopez.

❓ Quick check

After 5.Nxc6 bxc6 6.e5, which Black piece is attacked?

The e5 pawn attacks the knight on f6, so Black has to answer with ...Qe7 or ...Nd5.

Why play the Scotch?

The Scotch avoids the long theory of the Ruy Lopez and gives White a clear, open position. White's pieces develop easily, and Black has to be accurate to equalize.

White's plan

  • Develop quickly and castle kingside.
  • Use the space from the e5 pawn to cramp Black.
  • Target Black's split queenside pawns in the endgame.

Black's plan

  • Break with ...d5 or ...d6 to challenge the center.
  • Use the bishop pair and the open b-file.

The Classical line: 4...Bc5

Many Black players answer the Scotch by attacking the d4 knight with the bishop. White defends calmly and develops.

  • Delaying castling. The center opens early, so get your king safe.

❓ Quick check

After 3...exd4, how should White take back?

4.Qxd4?? loses the queen to 4...Nxd4. The knight takes back safely.

💡 Club-level tip

Many Black players at club level don't know the line, so you often get a big space advantage without having to memorize much.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Scotch Game good for beginners?

Yes. It opens the position early, the plans are easy to understand, and there's less theory than in the Ruy Lopez.

What is the Scotch Gambit?

In the Scotch Gambit, White plays instead of taking back on d4, offering the pawn for fast development and an attack on f7.
