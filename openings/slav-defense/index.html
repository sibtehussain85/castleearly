Slav Defense: a complete beginner's guide

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20266 min read

The Slav Defense answers the Queen's Gambit with 2...c6, supporting d5 with the c-pawn. Unlike in the Queen's Gambit Declined, Black's light-squared bishop isn't blocked by a pawn on e6, so it can come out to f5 or g4. It's a favorite of players who want solidity without passivity.

Quick answer

The Slav Defense starts . Black defends d5 with the c-pawn and keeps the light-squared bishop free to come out to f5 or g4. It's a solid answer to the Queen's Gambit that avoids the blocked-in bishop of the Queen's Gambit Declined.

How the Slav works

The pawn on c6 protects d5 just like ...e6 does in the Queen's Gambit Declined, but it doesn't block the c8 bishop. That's the big advantage. In return, the c6 pawn takes the best square from Black's queen's knight.

❓ Quick check

What's the Slav's big advantage over ...e6 (the QGD)?

...c6 protects d5 just like ...e6, but leaves the c8 bishop's diagonal open.

Black's plan

  • Develop the c8 bishop to f5 or g4 before ...e6.
  • Take on c4 when White's bishop is still on f1, and use the time to develop.
  • Free the position with ...c5 or ...e5.

The Exchange Slav

Some White players trade on d5 to get a symmetrical, quiet position. Black can simply develop: the position is equal.

❓ Quick check

In the Exchange Slav, what should Black watch out for?

Symmetry is fine for a while, but check every move for a tactic before copying.

Common beginner mistakes in the Slav

  • Playing ...e6 before developing the bishop. That turns the Slav into a passive QGD.
  • Trying to hold the c4 pawn at all costs. Grabbing it is fine; defending it with many pawn moves isn't.
  • Copying White forever in the Exchange Slav. Watch for the move where copying fails tactically.

💡 Club-level tip

Many club players don't know what to do after ...dxc4. Sometimes you can even keep the pawn with ...b5, but usually it's simpler to develop with ...Bf5, castle, and enjoy a solid game.

Frequently asked questions

Slav or Queen's Gambit Declined: which is better for beginners?

Both are excellent. The Slav keeps the c8 bishop free; the QGD keeps a slightly more solid center. Try both and see which positions you enjoy.

What's the Semi-Slav?

The Semi-Slav combines ...c6 and ...e6. It's very rich and can become sharp, so it's best learned after the basic Slav.
