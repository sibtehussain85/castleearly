Defense for Black · vs 1.d4

Dutch Defense: fight for e4 with the f-pawn

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

♟ Play this opening vs the Coach Free · every move explained

The Dutch Defense is the most aggressive reply to 1.d4. With the very first move Black's f-pawn takes control of e4 and announces a kingside attack. It leads to unbalanced positions where both sides can play for a win.

Quick answer

The Dutch Defense starts . Black controls the e4 square and plans to attack on the kingside. There are three main setups: the Stonewall (pawns on f5, e6, d5 and c6), the Leningrad (a bishop on g7) and the Classical (pawns on e6 and d6 with the bishop on e7). The price is a slightly weakened king.

The ideas behind the Dutch

Most defenses to 1.d4 fight for the e4 square with a knight on f6 or a pawn on d5. The Dutch does it with the f-pawn. That keeps Black's center pawns flexible and gives Black a half-open f-file later, but it also opens the diagonal from h5 to e8, so Black has to be a little careful about the king in the first few moves.

White's plan

  • Fianchetto with g3 and Bg2. The bishop is safe from Black's attack and presses on the long diagonal.
  • Against the Stonewall, aim for the hole on e5 with a knight and try to trade dark-squared bishops.
  • Open the center with e4 or play on the queenside with c4, b4 and c5.

Black's plan

  • Put a knight on e4.
  • Swing the queen to the kingside with ...Qe8 and ...Qh5.
  • In the Leningrad, prepare ...e5 to take over the center.

❓ Quick check

What is the main point of 1...f5?

The f-pawn guards e4, so White can't easily build a center with e4.

The Leningrad Dutch

In the Leningrad, Black combines the Dutch with a bishop on g7. The center stays fluid, and Black aims for ...e5. It is more dynamic than the Stonewall and a little harder to play.

Common mistakes in the Dutch

  • Chasing the bishop after 2.Bg5. After , taking the bishop with allows . Meet 2.Bg5 with a calm developing move such as ...g6 or ...Nf6.
  • Forgetting the weak diagonal. In the opening, always check for queen or bishop checks on h5 before you move your g-pawn or e-pawn.
  • In the Stonewall, ignoring the e5 square. White's knight wants to live there. Keep a bishop on d6 or a knight on d7 watching it.

❓ Quick check

Which setup has Black's pawns on f5, e6, d5 and c6?

Those four pawns form the wall that gives the Stonewall its name.

💡 Club-level tip

Start with the Stonewall. The pawn structure is always the same, your plan is clear (knight to e4, queen to the kingside), and you can add the Leningrad later when you want more variety.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Dutch Defense good?

It is sound and has been played by world champions such as Mikhail Botvinnik and Magnus Carlsen. It is riskier than the Queen's Gambit Declined, and in return it gives Black more winning chances.

Is the Dutch Defense good for beginners?

The Stonewall version is beginner-friendly because the setup is always the same. You do need to learn the early traps on the h5–e8 diagonal.

What is the Stonewall Dutch?

A Dutch setup with black pawns on f5, e6, d5 and c6. It locks the center and gives Black a strong outpost on e4.

What is the Leningrad Dutch?

A Dutch setup where Black plays ...g6 and ...Bg7, like a King's Indian with the f-pawn already advanced.

Why is it called the Dutch Defense?

It is named after the Dutchman Elias Stein, who recommended 1...f5 as the best reply to 1.d4 in a book published in 1789.

How do you play against the Dutch Defense?

The main line is g3 and Bg2 with c4 and Nf3. Sharper tries are the Staunton Gambit with 2.e4 and the sideline 2.Bg5.
