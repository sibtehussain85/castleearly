Danish Gambit: two pawns for two bishops

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

♟ Play this opening vs the Coach Free · every move explained

In the Danish Gambit White gives away not one pawn but two. In return both bishops land on long open diagonals pointing at Black's king before Black has developed a single piece. It is a wonderful weapon in fast games and a great way to learn what development is worth.

The ideas behind the Danish Gambit

White's idea is simple: every pawn Black takes costs Black a move, and White uses those moves to develop. After five moves White has both bishops on their best diagonals and Black has only moved pawns. If Black now plays slowly, threats against f7 and g7 arrive very quickly.

White's plan

  • Aim the bishops at f7 and g7.
  • Bring out the knights, castle and put the queen on b3 or the rooks on the open central files.
  • Open the game further with e5 when Black's king is still in the middle.

Black's plan

  • Play ...d5 to return material and develop.
  • Or decline early with 3...d5 and reach an equal position with no risk.
  • Don't keep both pawns and fall asleep: develop a piece with every move.

❓ Quick check

White is two pawns down after 5.Bxb2. What does White have in return?

White's bishops are developed and aimed at Black's king, while Black has not brought out a single piece.

How Black can decline the Danish Gambit

The most reliable answer is . Black hits the e4 pawn and opens lines for both bishops. White gets an isolated queen's pawn and Black has a comfortable game. This line is associated with José Raúl Capablanca.

Common mistakes in the Danish Gambit

  • As Black, taking both pawns and then playing passively. Moves like ...h6 and ...a6 lose quickly here. Every move must develop a piece or return material.
  • As White, thinking 7.Bxf7+ wins the queen. After , Black has , a discovered attack that wins the queen back.
  • As White, attacking before finishing development. The bishops are strong, but you still need your knights and rooks in the game before you sacrifice anything more.

❓ Quick check

What is Black's safest way to meet 3.c3?

3...d5 hits the center, opens lines for Black's bishops and avoids the gambit lines completely.

💡 Club-level tip

Play the Danish in blitz and rapid, where your opponent has no time to remember the defenses. If you would rather risk only one pawn, recapture on c3 with the knight on move four.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Danish Gambit sound?

With best play Black equalizes, either by returning the pawns with ...d5 or by declining with 3...d5. In practical games, especially fast ones, White's attack is very dangerous.

How do you beat the Danish Gambit?

The simplest way is 3...d5, declining the gambit. If you accept both pawns, play 5...d5 to give material back and finish your development.

Why is it called the Danish Gambit?

It was popularized by Danish players in the 1800s, most of all Martin Severin From, who played it at the Paris tournament of 1867.

Is the Danish Gambit good for beginners?

Yes, as a training opening. It shows very clearly how much open lines and a lead in development are worth.
