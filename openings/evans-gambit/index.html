Evans Gambit: the Italian Game with a pawn sacrifice

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

♟ Play this opening vs the Coach Free · every move explained

The Evans Gambit turns the quiet Italian Game into an attack. White offers the b-pawn to drag Black's bishop away, then uses the time gained to build a big center with c3 and d4. Garry Kasparov brought it back to the top level in the 1990s, and it remains one of the best gambits for club players.

The ideas behind the Evans Gambit

In the normal Italian Game White wants to play c3 and d4, but that takes time and Black is ready for it. In the Evans, the pawn on b4 forces the bishop to move twice. White gets c3 in with tempo, d4 comes one move faster, and the bishop on c1 can go to a3 or b2 because the b-pawn is gone.

White's plan

  • Build the center with c3 and d4.
  • Castle quickly and open lines while Black's king is still on e8.
  • Use Qb3 and Ba3 to attack f7 and keep the king in the center.

Black's plan

  • Develop with ...Nge7 and strike back with ...d5.
  • Don't take every pawn. After 7.O-O, capturing on c3 gives White a very dangerous attack.
  • Be ready to give the pawn back to finish development safely.

❓ Quick check

What does White gain for the b-pawn in the Evans Gambit?

The bishop has to move again after c3, so White builds a big center a move faster than in the normal Italian Game.

Why Black shouldn't take everything

If Black grabs a second pawn with , White's pieces come out with threats on every move. This is known as the Compromised Defense, and the name says it all.

Common mistakes in the Evans Gambit

  • As Black, taking the pawn on c3 as well. One pawn is fine; two leaves you too far behind in development.
  • As White, playing slowly. You are a pawn down, so every move should develop a piece or make a threat. If the position calms down, the extra pawn starts to count.
  • As Black, forgetting ...d5. It is the freeing move in almost every line. Without it White's center pushes your pieces back.

❓ Quick check

After 5.c3 Ba5 6.d4 exd4 7.O-O, which move is safest for Black?

7...Nge7 develops and prepares ...d5. Taking on c3 gives White a dangerous attack.

💡 Club-level tip

If you already play the Italian Game, the Evans is easy to add: the first three moves are the same. Learn the main line to move ten and the Qb3 and Ba3 ideas, and you will win a lot of short games.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Evans Gambit sound?

Yes. It has been played by world champions, including Garry Kasparov, and theory considers it fully playable. Black can reach a balanced game with accurate play.

Who invented the Evans Gambit?

William Davies Evans, a Welsh sea captain, who first played it in the 1820s.

How should Black respond to the Evans Gambit?

Accept with 4...Bxb4, retreat to a5 or e7 after 5.c3, and aim for ...Nge7 and ...d5. Declining with 4...Bb6 is a quieter option.

Should Black accept or decline the Evans Gambit?

Accepting is the main line and the best try for an advantage. Declining with 4...Bb6 is safe and avoids most of the theory.

What is the most famous Evans Gambit game?

The Evergreen Game, Anderssen against Dufresne in 1852. A well-known modern example is Kasparov's 25-move win against Anand in Riga in 1995.
