Defense for Black · vs 1.e4

Pirc Defense: let White build a center, then attack it

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20265 min read

♟ Play this opening vs the Coach Free · every move explained

The Pirc Defense breaks the first rule beginners learn. Black doesn't put a pawn in the center at all. White is allowed to build a big center with e4 and d4, and Black develops around it with a bishop on g7, castles, and only then strikes back.

Quick answer

The Pirc Defense starts . Black fianchettoes the bishop on g7, castles quickly and attacks White's center later with ...e5 or ...c5. It is a flexible, counterattacking defense named after the Slovenian grandmaster Vasja Pirc.

The ideas behind the Pirc

The Pirc is a hypermodern opening. The idea is that a big pawn center is also a target: pawns on e4 and d4 need protection, and Black can attack them from a distance with pieces and then with a well-timed pawn break. The bishop on g7 is the star, pointing at d4 and beyond.

White's plan

  • Take the space and develop behind the center.
  • Play Be3, Qd2 and Bh6 to trade off Black's best piece, often with a pawn storm to follow. This is the 150 Attack.
  • Or choose the aggressive f4 setup, the Austrian Attack, shown below.

Black's plan

  • Finish the setup: ...Bg7 and ...O-O.
  • Hit the center with ...e5 against quiet setups.
  • Hit it with ...c5 against the Austrian Attack.

❓ Quick check

Why does Black play 2...Nf6 before ...g6 in the Pirc?

With the knight on c3, White's c-pawn is blocked and can't support the center with c4.

The Austrian Attack: White's sharpest try

With White builds a three-pawn center and threatens to roll forward with e5. Black must react at once, and the right way is ...c5, attacking d4 before White is fully developed.

Common mistakes in the Pirc

  • Never striking back. If Black sets up and then waits, White's space advantage grows until Black is squeezed. You must play ...e5 or ...c5.
  • Allowing Bh6 for free. Against Be3 and Qd2, think about delaying castling or meeting Bh6 in a way that keeps your king safe. Your g7 bishop is your best defender.
  • Mixing it up with other openings. In the Modern Defense Black plays ...g6 and ...Bg7 before ...Nf6. The King's Indian has the same setup for Black but against 1.d4 with White's pawn on c4.

❓ Quick check

What is Black's best reply to the Austrian Attack setup with f4 and Nf3?

...c5 attacks d4 at once, before White can push e5 under good conditions.

💡 Club-level tip

The Pirc suits players who are happy to defend for a few moves and then counterattack. If you want simple, direct development as Black, the Caro-Kann is easier to start with.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Pirc Defense good?

Yes. It is a sound defense used by many grandmasters. White gets more space, so Black needs to know when to counterattack.

Is the Pirc Defense good for beginners?

It is harder than it looks, because Black has to play without a pawn in the center. Beginners usually do better with 1...e5 or the Caro-Kann first.

How do you pronounce Pirc?

"Peerts". It is named after Vasja Pirc, a Slovenian grandmaster.

What is the difference between the Pirc and the Modern Defense?

In the Pirc Black plays ...Nf6 early, which makes White defend e4 with Nc3. In the Modern, Black plays ...g6 and ...Bg7 first and keeps the knight back.

What is the best way to play against the Pirc?

The Austrian Attack with f4 and the 150 Attack with Be3 and Qd2 are the most dangerous. The Classical Variation with Nf3 and Be2 is the safest.
