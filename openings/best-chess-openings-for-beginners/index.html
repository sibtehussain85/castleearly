Opening guide

The best chess openings for beginners

By HussainUpdated October 29, 20268 min read

You don't need to know dozens of openings to play good chess. Most beginners only need three: one opening for White, one defense against 1.e4, and one against 1.d4. This guide shows the best choices, why they work, and how to pick the right one for your style.

The short answer: play the Italian Game or the London System with White, the Caro-Kann or Scandinavian against 1.e4, and the Queen's Gambit Declined against 1.d4.

First, learn the principles

Every good opening follows the same five rules. If you understand them, you'll find decent moves even when your opponent surprises you.

Best openings for White

  • Italian Game: the classic first opening. Quick development, early castling and a direct aim at f7.
  • London System: the easiest to learn: the same setup against almost anything Black does.
  • Four Knights Game: pure, principled development. Perfect for learning how openings work.
  • Queen's Gambit: more ambitious with 1.d4; teaches you a lot about central control.

Once you're comfortable, try the Ruy Lopez for deeper strategy, the Scotch for open positions, or the Vienna as a surprise weapon.

Best defenses against 1.e4

  • Caro-Kann Defense: rock-solid, with an active light-squared bishop and clear plans.
  • Scandinavian Defense: the simplest: you play almost the same setup every game.
  • Petrov Defense: for players who like 1...e5; very solid once you know the key trap.

If you want sharp, fighting games, the Sicilian is the most popular choice at every level, and the French suits patient counterattackers.

Best defenses against 1.d4

  • Queen's Gambit Declined: classical and solid, trusted by world champions for over a century.
  • Slav Defense: just as solid, with a bishop that gets out more easily.

Attacking players can try the King's Indian; strategic players will enjoy the Nimzo-Indian.

What beginners should avoid

  • Bringing the queen out early to chase quick checkmates. It works against total beginners, then stops working fast.
  • Memorizing long lines without understanding them. The first 6–8 moves and the plans are enough.
  • Switching openings every week. Stick with one choice for a few months so you learn its typical positions.

Frequently asked questions

How many openings should a beginner learn?

Three is enough: one for White, one against 1.e4 and one against 1.d4. Add more only when you're comfortable with those.

Should beginners play 1.e4 or 1.d4?

Both are fine. 1.e4 usually leads to open, tactical games, which are great for learning. 1.d4 leads to more strategic positions. The London System is a very easy way to start with 1.d4. If you prefer a quieter start with a side pawn, try the English Opening (1.c4).

What's the best chess opening of all?

There isn't one. All the main openings are sound. The best opening for you is the one whose positions you understand and enjoy.
