Deflection: force the defender away

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

Some defenders can't be captured, but they can be made to leave. Deflection gives a defending piece something more urgent to do, and while it is away, the thing it was guarding is yours.

Quick answer

A deflection is a tactic that forces an enemy piece to leave the square or line where it is doing an important defensive job. You do it with a check, a capture or a sacrifice that has to be answered. Once the defender has been pulled away, you take what it was protecting or give checkmate.

What is deflection in chess?

A deflection has two parts. First there is something you would love to do, such as a mate or a capture, that one enemy piece prevents. Second there is a forcing move that makes that piece go somewhere else. When both exist, the combination works.

The king is often the piece that gets deflected. A king that protects its queen can be pulled away by a check, and then the queen is simply taken.

How to spot a deflection

  • Name the defender. Work out exactly which piece is stopping your threat.
  • Look for a forcing move against it: a check it has to answer, an attack it must run from, or a sacrifice it has to capture.
  • Check what happens when it is gone. If your threat now works, you have a deflection.

How to defend against deflection

  • Don't give one piece a vital job with no backup. If your queen is protected only by your king, protect her again or move her.
  • Watch for checks and sacrifices aimed at your defenders, not only at your king.
  • Fix back-rank weaknesses. Many deflections work only because a rook is tied to the back rank.

Frequently asked questions

What is deflection in chess?

Deflection is forcing an enemy piece away from a square or line it needs to guard, so that you can win what it was protecting.

What is the difference between deflection and decoy?

A deflection pulls a piece away from a good square. A decoy lures a piece onto a bad square. Many combinations use both.

What is the difference between deflection and overloading?

An overloaded piece has two jobs and can't do both. A deflected piece may have only one job, and is forced to abandon it.
