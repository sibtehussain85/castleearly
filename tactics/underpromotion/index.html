Underpromotion: choosing a knight, rook or bishop

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

A pawn that reaches the last rank nearly always becomes a queen. Nearly. In a few positions a queen would spoil everything, and a knight or a rook is the only move that wins. Knowing those exceptions will save you half points.

Quick answer

Underpromotion is promoting a pawn to a knight, rook or bishop instead of a queen. There are two main reasons to do it: to make a knight move a queen can't make, usually a check or a fork, and to avoid stalemate, where a rook or bishop wins but a queen would leave the opponent with no legal move.

What is underpromotion?

The rules let you promote to any piece except a king, and you may have as many of each as you promote. A queen does everything a rook and a bishop can do, so there are only two situations where less is more.

The knight. A knight is the one piece that moves in a way the queen can't. If promoting to a knight gives check, and especially if it forks the king and queen, it can be far stronger than a new queen that the opponent would immediately answer with a check or a capture of their own.

The rook or bishop. A queen sometimes covers so many squares that the enemy king has no legal move left, which is stalemate and only a draw. A rook covers fewer squares, leaves the king a move, and still wins easily. The most famous case is the Saavedra position, a study from 1895 in which promoting to a rook is the only way to win.

When to think about underpromoting

  • Whenever promotion with a knight would be check. Look at what else the knight attacks.
  • Whenever the enemy king has very few moves. Check that a new queen doesn't stalemate it.
  • Whenever a new queen could be captured or forked at once, and a knight check would avoid that.

Common mistakes

  • Promoting to a queen on autopilot. Take one second to check for stalemate and for a knight fork.
  • Underpromoting for no reason. In the great majority of positions the queen is correct.
  • Forgetting the opponent can do it too. When you defend against a pawn on the seventh rank, check the knight promotion as well as the queen.

Frequently asked questions

What is underpromotion in chess?

Underpromotion is promoting a pawn to a knight, rook or bishop instead of a queen.

Why would you not promote to a queen?

Because a knight can give a check or fork that a queen can't, or because a queen would stalemate the opponent while a rook or bishop would not.

Can you promote to any piece?

You can promote to a queen, rook, bishop or knight of your own color. You can't promote to a king or leave the pawn as a pawn.

What is the Saavedra position?

A famous endgame study from 1895 in which White wins only by promoting a pawn to a rook, because promoting to a queen allows a stalemate defense.
