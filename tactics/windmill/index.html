The windmill: discovered check again and again

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

The windmill is the most destructive form of the discovered check. A rook and a bishop trap the enemy king in a loop: the rook captures something with a discovered check, comes back with a direct check, and then does it again.

Quick answer

A windmill (also called a see-saw) is a tactic in which one piece gives repeated discovered checks, capturing enemy material each time it moves away and returning with check to do it again. It is usually a rook on the seventh rank supported by a bishop on the long diagonal, against a king in the corner.

What is a windmill in chess?

The setup has three parts: an enemy king with almost no moves, your bishop aimed at it along a diagonal, and your rook standing on that diagonal next to the king. Every time the rook steps off the diagonal, the bishop gives check.

So the rook captures something along the rank, with discovered check. The king has one square to go to. The rook returns to its starting square with a direct check, the king has to go back, and the rook is free to capture something else. The cycle continues until there is nothing left worth taking.

The best-known windmill was played by the Mexican master Carlos Torre against the former world champion Emanuel Lasker in Moscow in 1925. Torre gave up his queen to set it up, and his rook then won back the queen with interest.

How to spot a windmill

  • Look for a king stuck in the corner with only one square to shuffle to.
  • Check for your rook on the seventh rank and a bishop on the diagonal behind it.
  • Make sure the king can't attack the rook or capture it when it returns with check.

How to defend against a windmill

  • Give your king a second escape square. The windmill only works while the king has to walk back and forth.
  • Challenge the bishop's diagonal before the rook arrives, by blocking it or trading the bishop.
  • Be suspicious of a free queen. A queen sacrifice near your king is often the start of a windmill.

Frequently asked questions

What is a windmill in chess?

A windmill is a sequence of alternating discovered checks and direct checks, in which a rook captures several enemy pieces while the king is forced back and forth.

Why is it called a windmill?

Because the rook swings out and back again and again, like the turning sails of a windmill. In English it is also called a see-saw.

What is the most famous windmill?

Carlos Torre's win against Emanuel Lasker in Moscow in 1925, where a queen sacrifice set up a rook and bishop windmill.
