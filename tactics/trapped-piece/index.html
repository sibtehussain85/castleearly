The trapped piece: attacked, with nowhere to go

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

You don't always need a fork or a pin to win material. Sometimes a piece wanders too far, you attack it, and it has no safe square to go to. That piece is trapped, and it is lost.

Quick answer

A trapped piece is a piece that is attacked and has no safe square to move to. Pieces on the edge of the board are trapped most often: a bishop that grabs a pawn on a2 or h2, a knight on the rim, or a queen that goes pawn hunting behind enemy lines.

Most common victims
Bishops and knights on the edge, greedy queens
What you win
Usually a piece for a pawn
Level
Beginner
Key habit
Count the escape squares before you attack

What is a trapped piece in chess?

Every piece needs squares to move to. In the middle of the board a knight has eight and a bishop up to thirteen, but on the edge those numbers drop quickly. A piece with few squares is easy to trap: take away its last escape square and then attack it, or attack it when it has none left.

The trapped piece doesn't have to be captured at once. It can't go anywhere, so you can often bring another attacker first and win it for nothing at all.

Classic traps to know

  • The greedy bishop. A bishop takes a rook pawn on a2, a7, h2 or h7, and the knight pawn steps forward one square to shut the door behind it.
  • The knight on the rim. A knight on the edge has only four squares, in the corner only two. A pawn can often cover all of them.
  • The Noah's Ark trap. In the Ruy Lopez, Black's queenside pawns advance with tempo and wall in White's bishop on b3.
  • The queen that goes pawn hunting. A queen that takes the b-pawn early can find every exit closed by developing moves.

How to spot a piece that can be trapped

  • Count its escape squares. If a piece has two or fewer safe squares, check whether you can cover them.
  • Look at enemy pieces on your side of the board or on the edge, especially after they capture a pawn.
  • Use pawns to trap. Pawn moves take squares away cheaply, and losing a pawn for a piece is a great trade.

How to avoid getting your pieces trapped

  • Before you take a pawn deep in enemy territory, find the way back. If the exit can be closed with one pawn move, don't go in.
  • Keep knights away from the edge unless they have a clear job there. "A knight on the rim is dim" is an old saying worth remembering.
  • If a piece is trapped anyway, get something for it. Capture a pawn before it dies; this is called a desperado.

Frequently asked questions

What is a trapped piece in chess?

A piece that is attacked and can't move to any safe square. It will be captured, usually for less than it is worth.

Which pieces get trapped most often?

Bishops that capture a pawn on the edge, knights on the rim, and queens that go hunting for pawns early in the game.

What is the Noah's Ark trap?

A trap in the Ruy Lopez where Black's a-, b- and c-pawns advance and trap White's light-squared bishop on b3.

Can the king be trapped?

A king that is attacked with no safe square is in checkmate. A king with no moves that is not attacked may be stalemated if no other piece can move.
