Double check: two checks with one move

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

A normal check can be answered in three ways: capture the checking piece, block it, or move the king. A double check takes away the first two. With two pieces checking at once, the king has to move, and if it can't, the game is over.

Quick answer

A double check is a check from two pieces at the same time. It happens when a piece moves, gives check itself and also uncovers a check from a piece behind it. A double check can't be blocked and the checking pieces can't both be captured, so the only legal reply is a king move.

What is a double check?

Every double check is a discovered check with something extra. One of your pieces steps off a line and uncovers a check from the bishop, rook or queen behind it, and the piece that moved lands on a square where it gives check as well.

Because the king is the only piece allowed to answer, the moving piece can go to a square where it is attacked. It can't be captured by anything but the king, and often not even by the king. That is why double checks appear at the end of so many famous attacking games, such as Richard Réti's win over Savielly Tartakower in Vienna in 1910.

How to spot a double check

  • Find a discovered check first. One of your pieces must be standing between your long-range piece and the enemy king.
  • See whether the front piece can check as it moves away. Knights and bishops are the usual candidates.
  • Then count the king's escape squares. If it has none, the double check is checkmate.

How to defend against a double check

  • Avoid the lineup. When an enemy rook or bishop points at your king through one of its own pieces, move the king or block the line before the discovery happens.
  • Give your king room. A double check is only fatal when the king has no safe square.
  • If it happens, look only at king moves. Nothing else is legal.

Frequently asked questions

What is a double check in chess?

A double check is when two pieces give check at the same time. It arises when a piece moves, gives check, and uncovers a second check from a piece behind it.

Can you block a double check?

No. One move can block only one line, and the other check would remain. The king has to move.

Can you capture a piece that gives double check?

Only with the king, and only if the square it captures on is not attacked. Capturing one checker with another piece would leave the king in check from the second.

Is a triple check possible?

No. Under the normal rules of chess, at most two pieces can give check after a single move.
