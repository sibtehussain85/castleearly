Removing the defender: take away the guard

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

A piece that is protected only once is safe only as long as its protector stays where it is. Capture that protector, or chase it away, and the piece behind it falls. This simple idea is behind a large share of the combinations in real games.

Quick answer

Removing the defender is a tactic where you capture, exchange or drive away the piece that protects a target, so the target can be taken or a checkmate can no longer be stopped. It is also called removing the guard or undermining.

Also called
Removing the guard, undermining

What does removing the defender mean?

Start with a target: a piece you attack, or a square where you would give mate. Then ask what is defending it. If the answer is a single piece, that defender becomes your real target.

There are two ways to remove it. You can capture it, even at the cost of a small sacrifice, because what you win next is worth more. Or you can attack it, usually with a pawn, so that it has to move and give up its job.

How to spot it

  • Find pieces that are attacked once and defended once. They depend completely on their defender.
  • Ask what stops your best threat. If one knight is all that prevents a mate, look for ways to capture or chase that knight.
  • Look for exchanges. Trading your bishop for the defending knight costs nothing if you then win a piece.

How to defend against it

  • Defend important pieces twice, or with a pawn, which is hard to remove.
  • Notice when one of your pieces has become the only guard of something valuable, and give it help before it is attacked.
  • Move the target if the defender is about to be exchanged.

Frequently asked questions

What does removing the defender mean in chess?

It means capturing or driving away the piece that protects something, so that the protected piece or square can be won.

What is the difference between removing the defender and deflection?

When you remove the defender you usually capture it. In a deflection you force it to move away to another square. The result is the same: the target is left unprotected.

Is it worth sacrificing to remove a defender?

Yes, when what you win afterwards is worth more than what you gave up, or when it leads to checkmate.
