The battery: two pieces on the same line

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

One piece attacking a square can be driven off or traded. Two pieces attacking it along the same line are much harder to stop. Lining them up like this is called a battery, and it is the simplest way to add force to an attack.

Quick answer

A battery is two or more pieces lined up on the same file, rank or diagonal so that they attack along it together. Two rooks on a file, a queen in front of a bishop on a diagonal, and a queen behind a rook are the most common batteries. The front piece attacks and the piece behind supports it.

What is a battery in chess?

When two pieces stand on one line, the back piece attacks through the front one. If the front piece captures something, the back piece protects it there. That is why a queen can capture a pawn right next to the enemy king and not be taken: the bishop or rook behind her is guarding the square.

Batteries also win simple counting battles. A pawn defended twice falls if you attack it three times, and doubling rooks on a file is the easiest way to add attackers.

The batteries you will use most

  • Queen and bishop on a diagonal. Aimed at h7 or g7, this is the classic mating battery against a castled king.
  • Doubled rooks. Two rooks on an open file take control of it and can invade the seventh or eighth rank.
  • Queen and rook on a file. Usually the rook goes in front so the more valuable queen stays safe behind it.
  • Alekhine's gun. Two rooks in front and the queen behind, all on one file. It is named after the world champion Alexander Alekhine, who used it to win a famous game against Aron Nimzowitsch in 1930.

How to build a battery

  • Pick the line first. Choose an open file or a diagonal that leads to a weak point, such as h7.
  • Decide which piece goes in front. On a diagonal aimed at the king the queen usually leads, so that she is the one who gives mate. On a file the rook usually leads.
  • Count attackers and defenders on the target square before you capture.

How to defend against a battery

  • Block the line with a pawn or a well-protected piece. Against a queen and bishop aimed at h7, ...g6 or ...f5 often closes the diagonal.
  • Trade one of the two pieces. A battery with one piece missing is just a single attacker.
  • Add defenders to the target square, such as a knight on f6 guarding h7.

Frequently asked questions

What is a battery in chess?

A battery is two or more pieces placed on the same file, rank or diagonal so they attack along it together.

Which pieces can form a battery?

Rooks and queens form batteries on files and ranks. Bishops and queens form them on diagonals.

What is Alekhine's gun?

A battery of two rooks with the queen behind them on the same file. It is named after Alexander Alekhine, who used it against Nimzowitsch at San Remo in 1930.

Should the queen be in front or behind in a battery?

It depends on the target. Behind a rook on a file she is safer. On a diagonal aimed at the king she is usually in front, so that she can deliver checkmate with the bishop protecting her.
