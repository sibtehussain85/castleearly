Back-rank mate: checkmate behind the king's own pawns

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

The back-rank mate catches a castled king that never got any breathing room. Its own pawns stand in front of it, a rook or queen lands on the last rank, and there is nowhere to run. It is the most common checkmate in games between beginners.

Quick answer

A back-rank mate is a checkmate given by a rook or queen on the opponent's first rank, where the king can't escape because its own pawns block the squares in front of it. It is also called a corridor mate. The simplest defense is to give the king an escape square, known as luft, by pushing a pawn such as h3 or ...h6.

What is a back-rank mate?

After castling, the king sits behind three pawns. They protect it from the front, but they also take away every square it could step forward to. If an enemy rook or queen reaches the back rank with check and nothing can capture it or block, the game is over.

The threat of a back-rank mate is often as useful as the mate itself. A rook that has to guard the back rank can't do anything else, so it can be overloaded or deflected, and pieces that look protected can sometimes be taken for free.

How to spot a back-rank mate

  • Check whether the enemy king has an escape square. Three unmoved pawns in front of a castled king is the warning sign.
  • Count the guards on the back rank. If only one rook or queen defends it, look for a way to remove, deflect or outnumber that defender.
  • Look for an open file your rook or queen can use to reach the last rank.

How to stop a back-rank mate

  • Make luft. Once your pieces are developed, a quiet pawn move like h3 (or ...h6) gives your king a flight square for the rest of the game.
  • Keep a rook on your back rank while enemy rooks and queens are still on the board.
  • Check your own back rank before grabbing material. Many players win a pawn with their last defender and get mated on the next move.

Frequently asked questions

What is a back-rank mate?

It is a checkmate delivered by a rook or queen on the first rank, where the king is trapped behind its own unmoved pawns.

How do you prevent a back-rank mate?

Give your king an escape square by moving one of the pawns in front of it, usually the h-pawn, or keep a rook defending the back rank.

What is luft in chess?

Luft is German for "air". It means a flight square for the king, made by pushing a pawn in front of the castled king so that it can't be mated on the back rank.

Can a queen give a back-rank mate?

Yes. Any piece that attacks along a rank can do it, which means a rook or a queen.
