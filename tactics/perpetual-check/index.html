Perpetual check: saving a lost game with endless checks

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

You are a rook down and your opponent is about to checkmate you. One check, and then another, and another, and the enemy king can never get away. The game is a draw. Perpetual check is the most common way to rescue a lost position.

Quick answer

Perpetual check is a situation where one player can give check forever and the other king can't escape the checks. The game ends in a draw. There is no separate perpetual check rule in modern chess: the draw comes from threefold repetition, the 50-move rule, or simply by agreement once both players see there is no way out.

What is perpetual check?

A perpetual needs two things. Your piece, usually the queen, must have a check available on every move. And the enemy king must have no way to stop the checks: no safe hiding square, no piece that can block with a gain of time, and no way to capture the checker.

The checks don't have to alternate between only two squares, but in practice they often do. The queen checks from one square, the king steps aside, the queen checks from another, the king steps back, and the same position comes round again. After it has occurred three times, either player can claim the draw.

The side giving perpetual check is normally the one that is losing. If you are winning, you should look for something better than a draw.

How to spot a perpetual check

  • Look at the enemy king's cover. A king with missing pawns in front of it is hard to hide from a queen.
  • Calculate every reply to each check. If any of them lets the king escape or blocks with a counterattack, there is no perpetual.
  • Before you resign or defend passively, list your checks. A draw is worth half a point.

How to avoid allowing a perpetual

  • When you are winning, keep your king safe first. One quiet move to shelter it is often better than grabbing more material.
  • Trade queens. Without the enemy queen, a perpetual is rarely possible.
  • Keep a piece ready to block a check with a threat, so the series of checks comes to an end.

Frequently asked questions

What is perpetual check?

It is an unstoppable series of checks. The king can't escape them, so the game is drawn.

Is perpetual check a draw?

Yes. Officially the draw is claimed by threefold repetition or reached by agreement, because the rules no longer list perpetual check separately.

How many checks make a perpetual check?

There is no fixed number. The game is drawn once the same position has appeared three times, or sooner if the players agree.

Is perpetual check the same as stalemate?

No. In stalemate the player to move has no legal move and is not in check. In a perpetual check the king keeps moving, but it can never get out of the checks.
