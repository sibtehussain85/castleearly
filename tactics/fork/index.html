The fork: one piece attacks two at once

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

The fork is the first tactic most players learn and the one that wins the most material. One of your pieces attacks two enemy pieces at the same time, your opponent has only one move, and so one of the two is lost.

Quick answer

A fork is a move where one piece attacks two or more enemy pieces at the same time. The opponent can usually save only one of them. Every piece can give a fork, but the knight fork is the most common, and a fork that includes a check is the hardest to escape.

What is a fork in chess?

A fork works because a player can only make one move at a time. When two of your opponent's pieces are attacked by the same piece, moving one leaves the other to be captured.

The most dangerous forks include the king. Check has to be answered immediately, so the other attacked piece is lost for certain. A knight that checks the king and attacks the queen at the same time is known as a royal fork. A knight that attacks king, queen and rook together is called a family fork.

Which pieces can fork?

  • Knight. It attacks in an L-shape that no other piece can copy, so the pieces it attacks can't attack it back. The squares c7 and f7 (c2 and f2 for Black) are the classic forking squares, hitting the king and a rook in the corner.
  • Pawn. A pawn attacks two squares diagonally in front of it. Pushing a pawn between two enemy pieces is the cheapest fork there is, because the pawn is worth less than anything it attacks.
  • Queen. She attacks in eight directions, so she forks often, typically a king and an undefended piece on the other side of the board.
  • Bishop, rook and king. Bishops fork along two diagonals, rooks along a rank and a file, and in the endgame even the king can attack two pawns at once.

How to spot a fork

  • Look for two targets a knight's move apart. Pieces on the same color squares, two or four squares apart, can often be hit by one knight.
  • Look for undefended pieces. A fork only wins material if at least one target can't be saved or defended.
  • Check every check. If a check also attacks something else, it is a fork.
  • Watch two pieces side by side with one square between them. A pawn can step up and attack both.

How to defend against forks

  • Keep your king and rooks off the knight's forking squares while an enemy knight is nearby, and guard c7 and f7 early in the game.
  • Don't leave pieces undefended. A fork on two protected pieces often wins nothing.
  • If you are forked, look for a move that saves one piece with a threat, such as a check or a capture. Sometimes that gains the time to save the other.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fork in chess?

A fork is one piece attacking two or more enemy pieces at the same time. The opponent can usually save only one, so the other is captured.

What is a royal fork?

A royal fork is a knight fork that attacks the king and the queen at the same time. The king must move out of check, and the knight then takes the queen.

Which piece is best at forking?

The knight. Its L-shaped move lets it attack pieces that can't attack it back, and it can jump over other pieces to reach forking squares.

Can a pawn fork?

Yes. A pawn attacks both squares diagonally in front of it, so it can fork two pieces. Pawn forks are very strong because the pawn is worth less than either target.

What is the difference between a fork and a double attack?

A fork is a double attack made by a single piece. Double attack is the wider term: it also covers cases where two different pieces create two threats at once, as in a discovered attack.
