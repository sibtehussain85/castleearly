The ladder mate: checkmate with two rooks

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

The ladder mate is the easiest checkmate to learn and one of the first every player should know. Two rooks walk the enemy king to the edge of the board, one rank at a time, and you don't even need your own king to help.

Quick answer

A ladder mate is a checkmate with two rooks, or a rook and a queen, that take turns: one piece guards a rank so the king can't come back, and the other gives check on the next rank. The king is pushed step by step to the edge of the board, where the final check is mate. It is also called the lawnmower mate or the rook roller.

Also called
Lawnmower mate, rook roller, staircase mate
Pieces needed
Two rooks, two queens, or a rook and a queen
Level
Beginner
Your king
Not needed

What is a ladder mate in chess?

Picture the board as a ladder of ranks. One rook stands on a rank and makes a wall the king can't cross. The second rook checks on the next rank, so the king has to step back. Now the first rook jumps past and checks on the following rank, and the pattern repeats until the king runs out of board.

The ladder mate step by step

  1. Cut the king off. Put one rook on the rank next to the king, on the side you want to push it away from.
  2. Check with the other rook on the rank the king is standing on. It must step back toward the edge.
  3. Leapfrog. The first rook now moves one rank past the second and gives the next check.
  4. If the king attacks a rook, swing that rook to the far side of the board along the same rank. The wall stays in place and the king can't reach it.
  5. Deliver mate when the king is on the last rank and the other rook guards the rank in front of it.

Things to remember

  • Keep the rooks far from the king and on different files, so the king can't attack them and they don't block each other.
  • The same method works along files. You can push the king to the a-file or the h-file just as easily as to the eighth rank.
  • With a queen and a rook, watch for stalemate. The queen covers so many squares that the king can run out of moves before you give check.

Common mistakes

  • Checking with the wrong rook. If the rook that was guarding the rank gives the check, the wall is gone and the king walks back toward the middle.
  • Leaving a rook next to the king. The king simply captures it. Move the attacked rook away along its rank first.
  • Giving pointless checks. Every check should push the king one step closer to the edge.

Frequently asked questions

What is a ladder mate?

A checkmate with two rooks, or a rook and a queen, that alternate checks to drive the king to the edge of the board rank by rank.

Why is it called the lawnmower mate?

Because the two pieces sweep across the board one row at a time, like a lawnmower cutting strips of grass.

Do you need your king for the ladder mate?

No. Two rooks, or a rook and a queen, can force mate on their own. That is what makes it the easiest basic checkmate.

How many moves does a ladder mate take?

Usually fewer than ten moves, even when the king starts in the middle of the board.
