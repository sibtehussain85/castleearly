The skewer: a pin in reverse

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

A skewer is the mirror image of a pin. You attack a valuable piece, it has to get out of the way, and you take the piece that was standing behind it. Skewers decide many endgames, especially those with rooks and queens on an open board.

Quick answer

A skewer is an attack on two pieces standing on the same line, where the more valuable piece is in front. It has to move, and the piece behind it is captured. Only bishops, rooks and queens can skewer. When the piece in front is the king, it is an absolute skewer, and the piece behind is always lost.

What is a skewer in chess?

For a skewer you need two enemy pieces on the same rank, file or diagonal with nothing between them. You attack the one in front. Because it is the more valuable piece, your opponent has to move it, and that uncovers the one behind.

The strongest skewers start with a check. The king is forced to step off the line, and whatever stood behind it falls. In queen endgames this is the standard way to win the enemy queen, so always look at checks along the line the king and queen share.

How to spot a skewer

  • Look for the king and another piece on the same line. A check along that line wins the piece behind.
  • Look for a queen in front of a rook, or a rook in front of a bishop or knight. Attacking the front piece with something cheaper wins material.
  • Open boards favor skewers. The fewer pawns there are, the longer the lines your bishops and rooks control.

How to defend against skewers

  • Don't line up your king with your queen or rook on an open file, rank or diagonal.
  • If you are skewered, look for a block: a piece or pawn that can step into the line, ideally one that is protected.
  • Move the front piece with a threat. If it gives check or attacks something bigger as it leaves, you may get time to save the piece behind.

Frequently asked questions

What is a skewer in chess?

A skewer is an attack on a valuable piece that has to move away, which lets you capture a less valuable piece standing behind it on the same line.

What is the difference between a skewer and a pin?

The order of the pieces. In a pin the less valuable piece is in front and is stuck. In a skewer the more valuable piece is in front and is forced to move.

Which pieces can skewer?

Only the long-range pieces: bishops, rooks and queens. Knights, pawns and kings can't, because they don't attack along lines.

What is an absolute skewer?

A skewer where the piece in front is the king. The king must move out of check, so the piece behind it can't be saved unless the check can be blocked.
