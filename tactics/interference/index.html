Interference: block the line between two pieces

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

Rooks, bishops and queens defend from a distance, which means they depend on open lines. Put one of your pieces on that line, and the defender is cut off from what it was guarding. That is interference.

Quick answer

Interference is a tactic where you place a piece on the line between an enemy piece and the square or piece it defends. The defender's line is blocked, so the thing it protected can be captured, or a pawn can promote, or a mate can no longer be stopped. The blocking piece is often sacrificed.

Also called
Interposition, obstruction
Works against
Rooks, bishops and queens defending along a line
Level
Intermediate
Often used for
Promoting a pawn or cutting off a back-rank defender

What is interference in chess?

A long-range defender has a weakness: every square between it and its target is a place where its line can be cut. If you can occupy one of those squares with gain of time, the defense collapses.

The blocking move usually needs to do something else as well, such as give check, attack a piece or threaten to promote. Otherwise your opponent just removes the blocker. In many cases the blocker is captured, but the capture pulls an enemy piece onto the line or costs a move, and that is enough.

How to spot interference

  • Find a target that is defended only from far away along a file, rank or diagonal.
  • Look at the squares in between. Can one of your pieces land on one with a check or a threat?
  • Think about passed pawns. Blocking the rook that guards the promotion square is one of the most common uses.

How to defend against interference

  • Defend important points from close by, or with two pieces on different lines.
  • Control the squares between your defender and its target, so a blocker can simply be captured without harm.

Frequently asked questions

What is interference in chess?

Interference is putting a piece on the line between an enemy piece and what it defends, so that the defender is cut off.

Is interference always a sacrifice?

Not always, but often. The blocking piece is frequently captured, and the time or line it cost the opponent is what makes the combination work.

What is the difference between interference and deflection?

A deflection forces the defender to move away. Interference leaves the defender where it is and blocks its line.
