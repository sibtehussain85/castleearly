Hanging pieces: the free material most games are decided by

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

A hanging piece is a piece that can be captured for free. Nothing fancy is needed to win it: you only have to notice it. At beginner level this is the most important tactic of all, because far more games are lost to a piece left hanging than to any clever combination.

Quick answer

A hanging piece is a piece that is attacked and not defended, so it can be captured for nothing. Chess players also say the piece is en prise. A piece that is defended fewer times than it is attacked is hanging too.

Also called
En prise, a loose piece
What you win
A whole piece or pawn for free

What is a hanging piece in chess?

Every piece on the board is in one of three states: safe, defended or hanging. A hanging piece has no defender, so the moment something attacks it, it can simply be taken. The English grandmaster John Nunn summed it up as "loose pieces drop off", and coaches repeat that line for a reason.

A piece can also hang when it is defended. Count the attackers and the defenders: if you attack a knight twice and it is defended only once, you win it. And if a pawn attacks a knight, it doesn't matter how many defenders the knight has, because you are happy to give a pawn for a knight.

How to spot a hanging piece

  • After every opponent move, ask what changed. Which piece did that move stop defending? Which new square does it attack?
  • Scan for pieces with no defender. Undefended pieces are targets even when nothing attacks them yet, because a fork or a discovered attack can hit them later.
  • Count attackers and defenders on any piece that is attacked. More attackers than defenders means it can be won.
  • Compare values. A defended piece is still hanging if a cheaper piece attacks it. (See piece values.)

How to stop hanging your own pieces

  • Do a blunder check before you let go of the piece. Look at the square you are moving to: can anything capture there?
  • Check what your moving piece was defending. Beginners often move a defender away and lose the piece behind it.
  • Keep your pieces protecting each other. A knight defended by a pawn is hard to win; a knight alone on the edge is a target.
  • Slow down after captures. Many hanging pieces appear in the move right after a trade, when the position has just changed.

Frequently asked questions

What does hanging a piece mean in chess?

It means leaving a piece where your opponent can capture it for free, because it is attacked and has no defender.

What does en prise mean?

En prise is French for "in a position to be taken". A piece that is en prise is attacked and can be captured, usually for free. It is pronounced roughly "on preez".

Is a defended piece ever hanging?

Yes. If it is attacked more times than it is defended, or if a less valuable piece attacks it, you still lose material when it is captured.

How do I stop blundering pieces?

Before every move, check which of your pieces are undefended and whether your move leaves anything attacked. This single habit removes most beginner blunders.
