The Greek gift: the bishop sacrifice on h7

By CastleEarlyUpdated November 16, 20263 min read

The Greek gift is the most famous attacking pattern in chess. White gives up a bishop on h7 to pull the black king out from behind its pawns, and a knight and queen arrive before the defenders can.

Quick answer

The Greek gift is the bishop sacrifice Bxh7+ (or ...Bxh2+ for Black) against a castled king. After Kxh7, White continues Ng5+ and then brings the queen to h5 or along the d1–h5 diagonal, with mating threats on h7. It is also called the classical bishop sacrifice.

Key moves
Bxh7+ Kxh7, Ng5+, Qh5
Pieces needed
Bishop on the b1–h7 diagonal, knight that can reach g5, queen that can reach h5
Level
Advanced
Also called
Classical bishop sacrifice

What is the Greek gift sacrifice?

The sacrifice works because three white pieces attack the king's position and, for a moment, only the king defends it. The bishop removes the h-pawn with check. If the king takes, the knight jumps to g5 with another check, and the queen follows.

When does the Greek gift work?

  1. Your bishop can capture on h7 with check, and the pawn is protected only by the king.
  2. Your knight can reach g5 safely. If a bishop on e7 or the queen on d8 can simply take it there, you need a pawn on h4 or a bishop on c1 backing it up.
  3. Your queen can get to h5, or to the d3–h7 diagonal, without delay.
  4. Black has no knight that can go to f6 to guard h7. A white pawn on e5 is what usually keeps it away.
  5. Black can't quickly defend h7 along the b1–h7 diagonal with a bishop or queen.

Black's three king moves

  • ...Kg8. The king goes back, and Qh5 threatens mate on h7. Black often has to give up material to stop it.
  • ...Kg6. The king comes forward. White keeps attacking with moves like Qg4, Qd3+ or h4–h5+, and has to calculate carefully.
  • ...Kh6. The king steps onto the diagonal of White's dark-squared bishop, and a knight move gives discovered check.

How to spot a Greek gift

  • Check the h7 pawn. Is it guarded by anything except the king?
  • Check f6. Is Black's knight missing from it, and can it get back?
  • Count your attackers. You need the bishop, a knight and the queen ready to join in within two moves.

How to defend against the Greek gift

  • Keep a knight on f6, or be able to bring one there, when White's bishop points at h7.
  • Don't let a pawn settle on e5 if it drives your knight away from the kingside.
  • Sometimes declining is best. After Bxh7+, ...Kh8 loses a pawn but can avoid the attack.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Greek gift in chess?

It is the bishop sacrifice Bxh7+ against a castled king, followed by Ng5+ and Qh5 with an attack on the exposed king.

Why is it called the Greek gift?

The name refers to the Trojan Horse, a gift from the Greeks that brought disaster to those who accepted it. Taking the bishop exposes the king in the same way.

Does the Greek gift always work?

No. It needs the right conditions, above all a knight that can reach g5, a queen that can reach h5, and no black knight able to defend from f6.

Can Black play the Greek gift?

Yes. The same idea with colors reversed is ...Bxh2+ followed by ...Ng4+ and ...Qh4.
